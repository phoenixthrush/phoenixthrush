<samp>I first got interested in coding in 2016 when a friend showed me a small Batch script with a Matrix-style effect. It was just a few lines printing random numbers in green, but somehow that was enough to get me hooked on programming. Back then, I spent a lot of time writing annoying little pieces of malware in Batch and VBScript, messing around with running scripts in the background, and testing actual malware in virtual machines just to watch them fall apart. Of all the things I wrote back then, I'm probably most proud of 666, which I wrote entirely in Batch and have since made public here on GitHub.</samp>

<samp>Since then, I've been going down one tech rabbit hole after another. My first PC was a prebuilt, but I've built every PC myself since then and replaced pretty much every part at least once. The only thing that somehow survived is the 1 TB Toshiba HDD from that first PC. It's almost ten years old now and still works, although these days it only holds Steam games because I'm pretty sure it's going to die eventually.</samp>

<samp>I spent a lot of that time messing around with Linux and installing what felt like every distro imaginable, from Ubuntu to Gentoo. No, I never tried Linux From Scratch, and at this point, I probably never will. I just don't have the same interest or motivation for that kind of thing anymore.</samp>

<samp>Eventually, I settled on Arch Linux as my daily driver. Part of that is because SteamOS is Arch-based, and some part of me keeps insisting that Proton must run better on Arch because of it, even though that's probably complete nonsense. I also just like having bleeding-edge software, up-to-date packages, and access to pretty much everything through the official repositories and the AUR. Besides, I've already got everything installed and configured the way I want it, and it all works, so why bother switching distros all the time?</samp>

<samp>All that distro hopping eventually got me into self-hosting and Linux system administration. I started running services on a Raspberry Pi and a VPS, and at one point I hosted quite a few Tor relays and open-source frontends like Piped, Invidious, Libreddit, and similar projects.</samp>

<samp>My first Pi was a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B with 4 GB of RAM. I've since moved everything to a Raspberry Pi 5 with 16 GB of RAM and a GeeekPi P33 M.2 NVMe M-Key PoE+ HAT, powered through a TP-Link TL-SG108PE switch. For storage, I use a dock with two 12 TB WD Red Plus HDDs in a Btrfs RAID1 configuration.</samp>

<samp>The current setup is connected through Tailscale with the Mullvad VPN add-on. I host Jellyfin, Pi-hole, Uptime Kuma, Beszel, Kiwix, qBittorrent, JDownloader, Sriracha, GitLab, Opengist, and an NGINX instance that serves directory listings for downloading files from mounted directories. I also host a few things I've written myself, including Belle, my own image gallery, my journal, AniWorld-Downloader, and a file-upload service. The latter three have public repositories here on GitHub.</samp>

<samp>While preparing for my exams, I also spent some time working with Cisco Packet Tracer. I don't use it anymore, but it helped me understand networking a lot better.</samp>

<samp>Most of what I know comes from online resources and a lot of trial and error. In the beginning, that mostly meant YouTube. These days, I usually just read the documentation or have an LLM summarize parts of it for me.</samp>

<samp>I mainly write Python and occasionally some C, although I rarely find myself writing C these days. When a project gets more complex, I sometimes reach for Rust instead. Having crates available is convenient in much the same way Python's libraries are, and embedding files is less annoying than it is in C. I'm still much more comfortable with C, though, and I've still got a lot to learn when it comes to Rust. It also seems like everyone is rewriting everything in Rust these days, which probably got me a little curious about it in the first place.</samp>

<samp>A lot of my personal projects involve web crawling and scraping with Python. Most of the time I spend coding probably goes into that and maintaining AniWorld-Downloader, which I've rewritten from scratch multiple times and which somehow keeps growing. Scraping websites can be quite fun sometimes, especially when you finally find that one thing you've been missing and everything starts working. I usually start by scraping the HTML directly and switch to browser automation using Patchright, a fork of Playwright, when that isn't enough or when it's simply easier to implement.</samp>

<samp>I like understanding how things work in detail, which often means rebuilding something that already exists just to figure it out. I've done that with sorting algorithms, hash algorithms, HTTP servers, HTTP proxies, Punycode, and full-adder logic, which I recreated in Minecraft lol. Knowing how to use something is useful, but I usually end up wanting to know why it works and what's happening inside it, too.</samp>

<samp>Reverse engineering is another interest I tend to pick up again every few years. Figuring things out is interesting on its own, but patching things and solving CTF challenges is also just fun. I mostly use Ghidra for that. Every now and then, I get back into messing around with my Digispark as a BadUSB device, bootloaders, payloads, wireless networking and security stuff like evil twin attacks and ARP poisoning, phishing sites, or writing annoying little pieces of malware. Sometimes I just like breaking things.</samp>

<samp>My main project is AniWorld-Downloader, which I started in April 2024. It's completely free and open source, and I want to keep it that way. No paid-only features, no ads, no tracking, no analytics. Just the code needed to get things done. Since then, it's grown to over 350 stars on GitHub, over 200k downloads on PyPI, and almost 100k pulls of its Docker image. Along the way, I've met a lot of people through it, and some of them have become close friends. Sometimes it started with an issue or a PR, and we just kept talking from there. Now we talk every day, hang out in voice chats, play games, and get to know each other's stories. One of the friends I met through the project lives about 650 km away, and I've made the trip there and back twice within a single year just to spend time with him. I never expected a downloader to lead to all these friendships and memories, but I'm really thankful it did. The Discord server keeps growing, and it means a lot that people stick around, help each other, and contribute their own ideas and code. I'm still working on the project, and I enjoy seeing what we can make of it together.</samp>

<samp>Outside of tech, I've been trying to take care of my mental health, and journaling has become part of that. I've been doing it almost every day for about a year now. It's helped me reflect on decisions I've made and think a bit more about the ones I'm making now and what I want to do in the future. I also tend to spend entire evenings playing metal on my guitar until it gets pretty late, or watching movies from my collection of Blu-ray rips on Jellyfin.</samp>

<samp>Music is a big part of my life, especially metal. I'm a metalhead, and some of the stuff I listen to gets pretty crazy. Some of my favorite bands and artists are System of a Down, A Perfect Circle, Type O Negative, Evanescence, Marilyn Manson, OOMPH!, Rammstein, Megadeth, Avenged Sevenfold, Trivium, As I Lay Dying, Falling in Reverse, Lamb of God, Gojira, Meshuggah, Ne Obliviscaris, Shadow of Intent, Infant Annihilator, Dimmu Borgir, Unlucky Morpheus, BABYMETAL, and many more.</samp>

<samp>I listen to a lot of Touhou music, too, especially Demetori. They're probably my favorite band of all time. I've been listening to the same albums for years and still haven't gotten tired of them. They went four years without releasing an album and then came back with another one in 2022. I'd love to own all their CDs one day, but they're ridiculously hard to find, and whenever they do show up on eBay, they're usually way too expensive.</samp>

<samp>Besides that, I listen to jazz, J-rock like Yousei Teikoku, and a lot of the metal I stumbled across through osu! maps. I used to play osu! for a few years but eventually stopped, so quite a bit of my music taste probably came from there.</samp>

<samp>When it comes to games, I mostly enjoy story-driven titles like NieR\:Automata, Life is Strange, Tomb Raider, Resident Evil, and Borderlands 2. I also occasionally play Minecraft, horror games, and co-op games like Phasmophobia, although these days that's mostly when friends ask me to join them. With friends, I'm usually happy to play whatever seems fun.</samp>

<samp>I'm not really into shooters. I'll play some Counter-Strike occasionally if friends ask, and I used to play a bit of PUBG, but these days I'm much more into visual novels and stuff like that. A friend recommended Eternum to me, and I absolutely loved it. It's so well done, and it got me into reading a few more gooner visual novels like Once in a Lifetime and Oppai Odyssey afterwards.</samp>

<samp>I used to watch a lot of anime, and I still keep track of everything I've watched on AniList, but at some point I just stopped watching anime altogether. I couldn't really tell you why. I just don't feel like it anymore. My favorite genre was obviously ecchi, but that was far from the only thing I watched, so don't get me wrong.</samp>

<samp>My favorite anime by far is NANA. She once told me she didn't think it would be something I'd like, probably because of its themes and the romance and stuff. She was completely wrong, though. I loved every moment of it and learned the opening song "rose" on guitar afterwards.</samp>

<samp>Speaking of guitar, I own two: an Ibanez GRX40-BKN and an Ibanez RG421QM-CBB. I usually play through either a Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 (4th Gen) audio interface or a Blackstar ID\:CORE Stereo 10 V3. I use Ableton Live with Neural DSP plugins like Archetype: Gojira, Archetype: Nolly, and the Fortin Nameless Suite, although these days I mostly stick to two of Nolly's factory presets, "Savage Rhythm" and "The Best Clean". I still kind of suck at playing, though.</samp>

<samp>As for where I want to go with all the tech stuff, I guess I just want to keep following those rabbit holes. It's probably the most useful skill I have when it comes to making money, so I might as well take it seriously. The good thing is that I actually enjoy learning this stuff, so it rarely feels like something I have to force myself to do.</samp>

<samp>I don't think I want to go much deeper into application development beyond my own projects and hobbies. I enjoy writing software when it's something I want to build, but I can't really see myself doing it every day for the next forty years. That sounds way too stressful to me. Linux system administration feels much more like something I could see myself doing long term, or maybe networking. I've also been getting more interested in containerization and container orchestration with things like Kubernetes and OpenShift. I haven't quite figured out which direction I want to go in yet, though.</samp>
